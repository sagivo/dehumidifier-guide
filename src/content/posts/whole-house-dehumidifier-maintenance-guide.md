A whole-house or crawl-space dehumidifier is a set-and-forget appliance — until the day it isn't. Capacity drifts down slowly as filters load up, drain lines grow algae, and coils collect dust, so the decline is easy to miss until the humidity numbers creep back up or the warranty claim gets denied. The good news: maintaining one takes minutes a quarter and almost nothing in parts. The bad news: neglect is one of the few things every major manufacturer explicitly ties to warranty coverage.

This guide covers the real maintenance routine for the brands that dominate this category — AprilAire, Santa Fe, and AlorAir — plus a seasonal schedule and the warranty fine print worth reading before you need it.

## Why maintenance matters more than on a portable

A portable dehumidifier that gets dirty just works a little harder for a while. A whole-house or crawl-space unit that's plumbed in and rarely seen has three failure modes portables don't: a drain line that backs up and triggers a float shutoff (or worse, spills), a condensate pump that fails silently in a sealed crawl space, and filter neglect that voids a five-year warranty on a $1,000+ machine.

The physics are simple. A dirty filter restricts airflow across the evaporator coil, which drops capacity and can cause frost buildup. A slimy drain line clogs, and since these units run continuously, even a slow backup means standing water somewhere you don't want it. And because the unit lives in a basement or crawl space you visit twice a year, the failure goes unnoticed for weeks. If you're comparing [stacking portables against one whole-house unit](/posts/portable-vs-whole-house-dehumidifier/), the maintenance story actually favors the big machine — fewer parts to touch, less often — but only if you do the few things it asks for.

## Air filter care, brand by brand

The filter is the maintenance item that matters most, and the three big brands handle it very differently.

**AprilAire E-series (E070, E080, E100, E130): washable MERV 8.** The stock filter washes out with warm water and mild detergent — shake off the excess, reseat it in the top and bottom rails, done. The unit's onboard control shows a "CLEAN FILTER" reminder every six months; you clear it by pressing the UP and DOWN arrows for three seconds. If you want better filtration, AprilAire sells optional MERV 11 upgrade filters (parts 5946 for the E070, 5947 for the E080/E100, 70000010 for the E130). A heads-up on shopping for replacements: some third-party manual mirrors list an E100 filter part number 5885 that we could not verify against any official AprilAire source — stick to the published upgrade numbers or contact AprilAire support before ordering.

**Santa Fe Ultra series (Ultra70/98/120): disposable MERV-13, 3–6 month cycle.** Santa Fe ships these with MERV-13 filtration, a real step up from the washable MERV 8 most competitors use — but it's a consumable, not a washable filter. Check it every three months and replace at least every three to six months; a dirty filter measurably cuts capacity and efficiency. Filter sizes vary by model (Ultra98/120 take 1.75"×14.00"×17.50"; the Ultra70 uses 1.75"×15.50"×19.50"), and the Ultra120V supports an optional MERV-14. Two things to take seriously: the manual says **"DO NOT operate the unit without the recommended filter"** and that **filter non-compliance voids the product warranty**. This is the strictest filter language in the category, so don't improvise with a cut-to-fit furnace filter.

**AlorAir Sentinel HDi90: slide-out washable mesh.** Unplug the unit, slide the filter out, vacuum it or wash it with warm water (no soap or solvents), and let it dry completely before reinstalling. There's no fixed replacement interval in the manual — clean it as needed, which in practice means quarterly in humid season. If you want stepped-up filtration, AlorAir sells staged parts: S-915 prefilter, S-917 MERV-8, S-918 HEPA, and S-919 carbon.

## Keep the condensate moving: drains and pumps

Filter neglect costs you capacity; drain neglect costs you water damage. If your unit drains by gravity — as most whole-house machines do — the drain line needs attention every one to three months during humid season, or at minimum every three to six months. The standard contractor practice: pour about a cup of distilled white vinegar into the drain line access point, let it sit around 30 minutes, then flush with water. Vinegar dissolves the algae and biofilm that grow in damp lines; a diluted bleach solution (about 1:10) also works but is harsher on fittings, so most pros keep it as the escalation option rather than the routine.

While you're down there, check the hose itself for kinks, cracks, and sagging sections where water can pool — our [drainage options guide](/posts/dehumidifier-drainage-options-gravity-vs-pump-vs-bucket/) covers what a correct gravity slope looks like and when a pump is the right answer.

Pump-equipped units need one more step. The [AlorAir Sentinel HDi90](/dehumidifiers/alorair-sentinel-hdi90/) has its pump built in, and its manual asks for pump-system cleaning **at least once per year** — drain pan, the hose into the pump, the pump reservoir, the float assembly, and the discharge tubing. Read the fine print: routine pump maintenance is **not covered by the unit's 1-year parts warranty** (only a genuinely defective pump is), so this one is squarely on the owner. It takes about fifteen minutes and it's the difference between a pump that lasts a decade and one that seizes in year three.

Santa Fe owners should know the Ultra98/120 supports an external low-voltage float switch or water sensor on the unit's FLOAT terminals — a cheap insurance policy in any install where an overflow would be expensive. Use a normally-closed switch so a failed sensor reads as a fault, not as all-clear.

## Coils, frost, and what to leave to the pros

AlorAir is the only one of the three brands that publishes a DIY coil-cleaning interval: clean the coils once a year with an approved self-rinsing foaming cleaner such as WEB Coil Cleaner. AprilAire and Santa Fe don't publish owner-manual coil procedures, so the practical rule for those brands is to keep the filter clean (which is what keeps coils clean) and let a tech handle the coils during annual HVAC service.

Frost on the evaporator coils deserves its own warning because it's the most common "something's wrong" symptom owners see. Ice forms when airflow is restricted by a dirty filter, when the refrigerant charge is low, or when inlet air is very cold. The fix that matters: **never chip the ice off**. Coil tubing is thin and puncturing it turns a service call into a dead unit — a permanent refrigerant leak. Power the unit down, let the ice melt naturally, clean or replace the filter, and see if the problem clears. If frost comes back, or the unit runs for hours and collects little water, you're looking at a possible refrigerant leak — and every manufacturer in this category is explicit that refrigerant work belongs to a qualified service technician. Santa Fe's manual lists "SERVICE MUST BE PERFORMED BY A QUALIFIED SERVICE TECHNICIAN" in caps, and says a lost charge must have the leak repaired and a new charge weighed in per the nameplate.

One thing not to chase: humidity sensor recalibration. No manufacturer we checked publishes a DIY sensor-calibration procedure. Santa Fe's own troubleshooting lists your external humidity meter being out of calibration as a possible cause of "underperformance" — meaning if the numbers look wrong, verify with a second meter before assuming the dehumidifier is at fault. If two meters agree the unit isn't hitting setpoint, treat it as a service call, not a screwdriver job.

## A seasonal schedule you can actually follow

Pin this to the mechanical room door:

- **Spring startup (March/April):** Clean or replace the filter. Flush the drain line with vinegar. Verify the unit restarts and the pump (if equipped) cycles. Confirm the humidistat setpoint — most homes want [45–50% relative humidity](/posts/ideal-indoor-humidity-levels-summer-winter/) in summer.
- **Mid-summer check (July):** Quick filter inspection (this is peak load — a filter that looks loaded at six months often looks loaded at four). Glance at the drain discharge to confirm water is actually flowing during a run.
- **Fall (October):** Filter clean or replace. Drain-line flush. If the crawl space is sealed and the unit runs year-round, this is the visit to do the annual AlorAir pump-reservoir cleaning.
- **Winter (January, if the unit runs):** Filter check only. Some basements stay damp all winter; don't assume the unit is idle just because it's cold — check the [energy cost math](/posts/whole-house-dehumidifier-energy-cost-per-month/) before you switch it off for the season.

That rhythm — filters quarterly, drains seasonally, pump annually — covers everything any of the three manufacturers ask of an owner. It also happens to be the entire maintenance content of most warranty fine print: "provide normal care and maintenance (including, but not limited to cleaning filters, coils and pumps)."

## The warranty fine print, summarized

Warranties in this category are generous but conditional, and maintenance sits in the conditions.

**AprilAire: 5-year limited warranty** from date of purchase on the current E-series, covering defects in materials and workmanship (replacement part or unit at their discretion). Excluded: filters and consumables, labor, shipping, removal and reinstallation, installer-furnished materials, and — the one that matters here — damage from misuse, abuse, **improper installation, or failure to maintain per the instructions**. Older manuals add that the warranty is void if defects result from the unit not being installed by a qualified HVAC contractor.

**Santa Fe (Therma-Stor): 5-year product warranty plus 6-year component warranty** under the current terms effective January 1, 2024 (from purchase by the original customer through Therma-Stor or an authorized reseller). Pre-2024 manuals show a shorter 2-year product + 6-year component structure, so check the paperwork that came in the box with your specific unit. Excluded: refrigerant, piping, supplies, transportation, and labor after year two. And the filter rule again: non-compliance voids the product warranty.

**AlorAir: tiered coverage.** Six months parts and labor including shipping, then one year parts and labor (return shipping on you), then three years parts and labor on the refrigeration system only, then five years parts-only on the refrigeration system. Original owner only, non-transferable. Customer responsibilities explicitly include cleaning filters, coils, and pumps — and routine pump maintenance is excluded from the 1-year parts coverage.

The pattern is obvious: all three tie coverage to owner-performed maintenance, and none of them cover consumables or neglect. Keep a paper trail — a dated photo of the cleaned filter and a note of the drain flush takes thirty seconds and makes any future warranty conversation much easier.

## What a tech visit actually costs

If you'd rather outsource the routine, a dedicated annual dehumidifier service runs roughly $150–$300, and it's often cheaper bundled with an HVAC maintenance visit (a standard HVAC tune-up runs about $75–$200). A one-time diagnostic call for a real problem — not collecting water, error codes, persistent frost — typically costs ~$70–$200. Replacement filters run ~$15–$40; everything else in the routine is essentially free.

The repair math worth knowing: a refrigerant repair on an aging unit can run around $900, which is usually not economical against the cost of replacement. That's the real argument for the maintenance routine above — nothing in it costs much, and the alternative is replacing a four-figure machine years early.
