Square footage alone doesn't size a dehumidifier. A 2,500 sq ft house in Houston and the same house in Denver have radically different moisture loads, because the air infiltrating the house — through every crack, vent, and doorway — carries the local climate with it. This guide gives you zone-by-zone starting points, the multipliers that adjust them, and the headroom rule that keeps you out of trouble.

All capacities below are **AHAM pints** (80°F/60% RH). If you're looking at a unit marketed in saturation pints, decode it first with [AHAM vs saturation pints explained](/posts/aham-vs-saturation-pints-explained/) — feeding saturation numbers into these tables will undersize your system.

## The base formula

**Base AHAM pints = (square footage ÷ 500) × 10**, in humid climates.

That's ~10 pints per 500 sq ft: a 2,000 sq ft home starts at 40, a 3,000 sq ft home at 60. Then you adjust for zone, dampness, and headroom. In arid climates, start at roughly half that and treat only the zones that actually need it.

## Climate zone sizing tables

### Gulf Coast & Deep South (Houston, New Orleans, Jacksonville, coastal TX/LA/FL)

Year-round humidity, long cooling seasons, frequent 70°F+ dewpoints. Size aggressively.

| Home size | Base pints | Recommended AHAM (with headroom) | Typical pick |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1,500 sq ft | 30 | 40–55 | AlorAir HD55 |
| 2,500 sq ft | 50 | 70–80 | AprilAire E070/E080 |
| 3,500 sq ft | 70 | 90–100 | AprilAire E100 Pro, AlorAir HDi90 |
| 5,000+ sq ft | 100 | 120–130+ | AprilAire E130 |

Gulf Coast rule: **round up a full unit size** from what the base formula says. A unit that's "about right" in Ohio is undersized in Louisiana.

### Southeast inland (Atlanta, Charlotte, Nashville, Birmingham)

Hot humid summers, milder winters with real shoulder-season humidity. The base formula works, with headroom.

| Home size | Base pints | Recommended AHAM (with headroom) | Typical pick |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1,500 sq ft | 30 | 40–55 | AlorAir HD55 |
| 2,500 sq ft | 50 | 60–70 | Santa Fe Compact70, AprilAire E070 |
| 3,500 sq ft | 70 | 80–100 | AprilAire E080/E100 Pro |
| 5,000+ sq ft | 100 | 100–130 | AprilAire E100 Pro/E130 |

### Mid-Atlantic (DC, Baltimore, Philadelphia, Richmond)

Humid summers, genuinely dry winters. Size for the summer peak; the unit will loaf November–March, which is fine.

| Home size | Base pints | Recommended AHAM (with headroom) | Typical pick |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1,500 sq ft | 30 | 40–55 | AlorAir HD55 |
| 2,500 sq ft | 50 | 60–70 | AprilAire E070, Santa Fe Compact70 |
| 3,500 sq ft | 70 | 80–90 | AprilAire E080, AlorAir HDi90 |
| 5,000+ sq ft | 100 | 100–130 | AprilAire E100 Pro/E130 |

### Midwest (Chicago, Minneapolis, Kansas City, Indianapolis)

Intense but shorter humid season; basements are the main battleground. Base formula minus a touch for living space, plus for basements.

| Home size | Base pints | Recommended AHAM (with headroom) | Typical pick |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1,500 sq ft | 30 | 40–55 | AlorAir HD55 |
| 2,500 sq ft | 50 | 55–70 | AprilAire E070, Santa Fe Compact70 |
| 3,500 sq ft | 70 | 80–100 | AprilAire E080/E100 Pro |
| 5,000+ sq ft | 100 | 100–130 | AprilAire E100 Pro/E130 |

Midwest note: if the problem is specifically a basement (the classic Midwest case), consider sizing a dedicated unit for the basement zone rather than upsizing the whole-home unit — see the [crawl space guide](/posts/crawl-space-dehumidifier-guide/) for zone thinking.

### Arid & Mountain West (Denver, Phoenix, Salt Lake City)

Whole-home dehumidification is rarely needed. Size for the specific damp zone — usually a basement — at roughly half the humid-climate base.

| Zone size | Recommended AHAM | Typical pick |
|---|---|---|
| Basement up to 1,500 sq ft | 30–55 | AlorAir HD55 |
| Basement 1,500–2,500 sq ft | 55–70 | Santa Fe Compact70, AprilAire E070 |

Don't buy whole-home capacity for a basement problem in an arid climate. You'll pay for pints you'll never use.

## Dampness multipliers

Apply these to your zone-adjusted number:

| Condition | Multiplier |
|---|---|
| Vented (unencapsulated) crawl space | × 1.5 |
| Damp basement (seepage history, musty) | × 1.25–1.3 |
| Older leaky construction (pre-1980, little air sealing) | × 1.15–1.25 |
| Tight new construction | × 0.9 |
| Encapsulated crawl space | × 0.8–0.85 |
| Indoor pool or spa | × 1.5+ (and get pool-rated advice) |

Example: a 2,500 sq ft Southeast home (base 50, zone-adjusted 60–70) with a damp basement: 65 × 1.25 ≈ 81 → buy an 80–90 AHAM unit (AprilAire E080 or AlorAir HDi90).

## The 20–30% headroom rule

After zone and dampness adjustments, multiply by **1.2–1.3** and buy at or above the result. Why:

- **Heat waves happen.** Design-week humidity exceeds average-week humidity; headroom covers it.
- **Efficiency.** A unit running at 70% capacity uses less energy per pint than one redlined.
- **Longevity.** Compressors that cycle normally outlast compressors that never shut off.
- **Noise.** A loafing unit is quieter than a straining one.

Headroom is not "buy double." One step up — E080 instead of E070, E100 Pro instead of E080 — is the right magnitude. Two steps up wastes money and can short-cycle.

## Worked example

3,000 sq ft home in Charlotte, 1970s construction, damp basement:

1. Base: (3,000 ÷ 500) × 10 = **60 pints**
2. Southeast zone: 60 sits in the 60–70 band → **65**
3. Older leaky construction: 65 × 1.2 = **78**
4. Damp basement: 78 × 1.25 = **97.5**
5. Headroom × 1.25: 97.5 × 1.25 ≈ **122**

Buy: AprilAire E130 (130 AHAM) — or encapsulate/seal first and re-run the math, which might land you at the E100 Pro instead. That's the value of doing the arithmetic: it tells you whether air-sealing work saves you a unit size.
