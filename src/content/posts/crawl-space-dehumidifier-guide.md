The crawl space is where humidity goes to cause the problems you discover too late: mold on floor joists, musty air wicking up into the living space, wood rot, and the damp smell that no air freshener fixes. It's also the hardest dehumidification job in a typical home — low, often vented to outside air, usually with no drain and no outlet nearby. This guide covers the setup that actually works, then the units built for it.

## Why crawl spaces need dedicated units

A portable 50-pint dehumidifier is designed for a room: flat floor, nearby outlet, a bucket you empty, moderate temperatures. A crawl space offers none of that. The moisture load is higher (ground moisture wicks up constantly, vents admit humid outside air), the volume of water removed is larger (gallons per day, not a bucket), there's typically no floor drain for gravity drainage, and clearances can be under 18 inches.

A dedicated crawl-space or whole-house unit differs in the ways that matter: higher AHAM capacity, **condensate pump options** (built-in or external), low-profile designs that fit tight spaces, and construction meant to run for years in a harsh environment. This is not a place to economize with the wrong category of machine.

## Encapsulation basics: cut the load before you size the unit

**Encapsulation** means converting a vented crawl space into a sealed, conditioned space:

1. **Seal the vents.** Vented crawl spaces in humid climates are essentially outdoor air with a roof — closing the vents is the single biggest load reduction.
2. **Lay a heavy vapor barrier.** 20-mil reinforced polyethylene across the entire floor, sealed at seams and run up the foundation walls. This stops ground moisture at the source.
3. **Seal gaps and penetrations.** Rim joists, pipe penetrations, the access door — every air leak is a humidity leak.
4. **Condition the space.** The dehumidifier (and sometimes a small supply of conditioned air) keeps the sealed space at 45–55% RH.

An encapsulated crawl space can cut the dehumidification load by half or more versus a vented one. That means a smaller unit, lower purchase price, and lower running costs for the life of the home. If you're deciding between encapsulation and a bigger dehumidifier, do the encapsulation — no unit outruns an open vent in August.

For sizing context: a 1,500 sq ft **encapsulated** crawl in a humid climate typically needs roughly 40–55 AHAM pints (an HD55 or E070-class unit); the same space **vented** can need 70–90+. See [sizing by climate zone](/posts/dehumidifier-sizing-by-climate-zone/) for the full tables.

## Drainage and pump planning

Assume there is no gravity drain. In a crawl space, the unit sits at the lowest point of the house — water doesn't flow uphill to a drain that doesn't exist. Your options:

- **Built-in pump (recommended):** The **AlorAir Sentinel HDi90** ships with an integrated condensate pump — water leaves through a small vinyl tube you route to daylight, a sump pit, or an exterior drain. One unit, no extra parts, no extra failure points beyond the pump itself.
- **External condensate pump:** Any unit can be paired with an aftermarket pump, adding cost and one more mechanical part to maintain.
- **Sump pit tie-in:** If the crawl already has a sump for groundwater, the dehumidifier can often drain to it — check local code on combining flows.

Route the discharge line with a continuous downhill slope or let the pump do the lifting, protect exterior terminations from freezing in cold climates, and make the line inspectable — a clogged condensate line is the most common "my dehumidifier stopped working" service call.

## Low-clearance fit

Measure before you buy. Crawl space clearances vary wildly — 36 inches in newer construction, under 16 in older homes — and the unit needs service access around it, not just a slot it barely fits into.

- **Santa Fe Compact70:** 12 inches tall, the purpose-built answer for tight crawls. 70 AHAM pints, MERV-13 filtration, 6-year warranty, ~$1,300–1,450.
- **AlorAir HDi90 / HD55:** Low-profile horizontal designs that fit typical crawl installations; verify your specific clearance against the spec sheet.
- **AprilAire E070 Pro:** A compact whole-house unit (70 AHAM, 2,200 sq ft, ~$1,550–1,770) that works in crawls with adequate clearance and pairs well with ducted setups.

## Setup checklist

1. Encapsulate first (or at minimum seal vents + lay the vapor barrier).
2. Size in AHAM pints with headroom — vented crawls size up significantly.
3. Confirm clearance with a tape measure, not a guess.
4. Plan the drain route and pump before the unit arrives.
5. Set the humidistat to 50–55% RH — lower wastes energy without meaningful benefit.
6. Check the condensate line seasonally; check the filter per the manual.
