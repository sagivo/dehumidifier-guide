The most common dehumidifier mistake isn't buying the wrong whole-house unit — it's buying a portable for a whole-house job. A $300 portable feels like the economical choice until you're emptying buckets in three rooms, the crawl space is still growing mold, and the electric bill shows three compressors running around the clock. Here's the honest comparison.

## Head-to-head

| | Portable (50-pint class) | Whole-house |
|---|---|---|
| Upfront cost | ~$250–350 | ~$506–2,600 (plus install if ducted) |
| Coverage | One room, ~500–1,000 sq ft | Whole home, basement, or crawl space |
| Drainage | Bucket (manual) or small hose | Gravity drain or condensate pump — gallons/day handled automatically |
| Efficiency | Standard; worse per pint | Energy Star common; Most Efficient available (AprilAire E080/E100/E130) |
| Lifespan/warranty | Typically 1–2 yr warranty; 3–5 yr realistic life | 5–6 yr warranties; 8–12+ yr realistic life |
| Noise | In your living space | In basement/crawl/utility room — out of earshot |
| Crawl space capable | No | Yes (dedicated models: HDi90, Compact70) |
| Ducted/HVAC integration | No | Yes |

## When a portable is enough

Buy the portable when the problem is genuinely small and contained:

- **One damp room** — a musty guest bedroom, a humid laundry room, a single basement room with the door closed.
- **Renters** — you can't install a whole-house unit in a home you don't own.
- **Temporary problems** — post-flood dry-out, a single wet season, new-construction moisture burn-off.
- **Supplemental duty** — one stubborn room in an otherwise well-controlled house.

A 50-pint portable (~$250–350) in a single room it can actually close off is the right tool at the right price. No argument.

## When a portable is false economy

The math flips the moment the job outgrows one room:

- **Multiple problem areas.** Two portables ($500–700) running simultaneously already approach the price of an AlorAir HD55 (~$506–638) — a whole-house-class unit with better efficiency and automatic drainage. Three portables and you've spent AprilAire E070 money for worse coverage.
- **Crawl spaces.** A portable cannot do this job — no pump, no capacity, wrong construction. Period. See the [crawl space guide](/posts/crawl-space-dehumidifier-guide/).
- **Whole-home humidity.** A portable in the living room does nothing for the bedrooms with the doors closed, and nothing for the basement feeding moisture upward. Humidity is a whole-house problem or it isn't; one room's unit can't solve a house's problem.
- **Bucket logistics.** A portable pulling real moisture fills its bucket daily or more. Gravity-hose it to a drain and you've solved the bucket but still have a single-room unit doing a multi-room job.

## The 5-year cost picture

Upfront price favors the portable; everything else favors the whole-house unit:

- **Energy:** Whole-house units remove more pints per kWh. Two or three portables running to cover a house draw more combined power than one efficient whole-house unit — and Energy Star Most Efficient models widen the gap further. Model it in the [operating cost tool](/tools/operating-cost/).
- **Replacement:** Portables realistically last 3–5 years; whole-house units 8–12+. Over a decade you may buy two or three portables versus one whole-house unit.
- **Your time:** Buckets, filter cleanings across multiple units, moving portables between rooms — the hidden cost nobody prices.

Rough 5-year sketch for a 2,500 sq ft humid-climate home: three portables (~$900 upfront, replaced once = ~$1,800, higher energy, partial coverage) versus one AprilAire E080 (~$1,750–1,900 installed standalone, lower per-pint energy, full coverage, 5-yr warranty). The whole-house unit wins on cost *and* results. Run your own numbers in the [portable vs whole-house tool](/tools/portable-vs-whole-house/).

## The crossover rule

**If you're considering a second portable, price a whole-house unit instead.** One portable is a room solution. Two portables is a whole-house problem wearing a room-solution costume — and the costume costs almost as much as the real solution while covering less.

The AlorAir HD55 (~$506–638, 55 AHAM pints) exists precisely for this crossover: whole-house-class capacity and drainage at barely above two-portable money. From there, the [2026 rankings](/posts/best-whole-house-dehumidifiers-2026/) step up through mid-range and premium options.

## Installation: what each path actually involves

A portable's "installation" is unboxing it and finding an outlet — which is genuinely part of its appeal. A whole-house unit asks more of you, and it's worth knowing what before you decide:

- **Standalone whole-house** (AlorAir HD55/HDi90, Santa Fe Compact70, AprilAire E070): place it, plug it into a standard outlet, connect the drain (gravity hose or pump line). A handy homeowner can do this in an afternoon; the drain routing is the only part that requires planning.
- **Ducted whole-house** (typically AprilAire E-series): ties into your HVAC ductwork, usually needs a licensed contractor, and the labor is a meaningful line item on top of the unit. In exchange you get even whole-home coverage and the unit lives out of sight.

Don't let installation intimidate you out of the right category — a standalone whole-house unit is barely more work than a portable with a drain hose, and it solves a fundamentally bigger problem. The most expensive dehumidifier is the one you buy twice: first the portable that couldn't do the job, then the whole-house unit you should have bought.

## Bottom line

Portables are excellent single-room tools and terrible whole-house strategies. Match the machine to the size of the problem: one room, one portable; a house, a basement, or a crawl space, a whole-house unit sized in AHAM pints with proper drainage.
