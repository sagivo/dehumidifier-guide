The AprilAire E100 and E130 sit at the top of AprilAire's residential E-Series, and they cause more buyer paralysis than any other pair in the category. They're the same brand, the same design language, the same efficiency badge — separated by 30 pints and roughly $450. Here's how to decide in about five minutes.

Read our full [AprilAire E100 Pro review](/dehumidifiers/aprilaire-e100-pro/) and [AprilAire E130 review](/dehumidifiers/aprilaire-e130/) for the deep dives. If you're still torn, the tiebreaker most buyers land on is simple: measure your install space and check your climate zone. If the E130 fits and you're in a humid region, the extra headroom is worth the premium; if space is tight or your climate is moderate, the E100 is the smarter buy.

## What "85 vs 105 DOE pints" means in practice

Both units carry two numbers — the marketed pints and the DOE integrated rating — and you should size on the DOE figure. (If the two-number system is new to you, our [AHAM vs saturation explainer](/posts/aham-vs-saturation-pints-explained/) covers why it exists.)

The practical gap is 20 DOE pints: meaningful, but not transformative. In a moderately humid climate, that's roughly the difference between covering a 4,500 sq ft home comfortably and covering a 6,000 sq ft home comfortably. It is not the difference between solving and not solving a moisture problem — if 85 pints won't touch your humidity, 105 probably won't either, and the issue is air sealing or drainage, not capacity.

## Size and weight: the underrated difference

The spec people skim past is dimensions. The E130 is **19.5" × 18.75" × 30" and 98 pounds** — nearly 50% heavier than the E100's 64 lbs, and a meaningfully bigger box to maneuver into a crawl space or attic. If your install location is a tight crawl space with a small access hatch, measure twice: the E130's footprint has ended more than one purchase. The E100's 14" × 15" × 26" cabinet fits where the E130 won't.

Both can be had in caster variants (E100C, E130C) for freestanding/portable placement, and hardwired variants (E100H, E130H) for permanent installs.

## Efficiency and running costs: closer than they look

Same 2.35 IEF, same R-32 refrigerant, same Energy Star Most Efficient 2025 badge. The E130 draws more power (8.3A vs 6.9A) because it moves more air and water, but per pint removed they're equally efficient. DOE rated annual consumption: 821 kWh/yr (~$145/yr) for the E100, 926 kWh/yr (~$164/yr) for the E130 — about a $19/year difference at typical rates. Over a 10-year life, the running-cost gap is under $200, which is noise next to the $450 purchase-price gap. See our [energy cost guide](/posts/whole-house-dehumidifier-energy-cost-per-month/) for the full math.

## Who should buy the E100

- Homes up to ~5,000–5,500 sq ft in moderate to humid climates
- Basements and crawl spaces where the E130 physically won't fit
- Buyers who want the best value per pint in AprilAire's line
- Anyone stepping up from an E080 who needs one more tier of headroom

The E100 is the default recommendation for "large home, normal situation." It's the unit our [best whole-house dehumidifiers](/posts/best-whole-house-dehumidifiers-2026/) ranking picks as best overall for a reason: it covers the broadest range of real homes at the sanest price.

## Who should buy the E130

- Homes over ~5,500 sq ft, or over ~4,000 sq ft in Gulf Coast / deep-Southeast humidity
- Homes with a known moisture load: wet crawl space, indoor pool, lots of occupants, tight new-build envelope in a humid zone
- Buyers between sizes who follow the size-up rule (most buyers, honestly)
- Installations with room for a 30"-long, 98-lb cabinet

## Installation and placement compared

Both units install the same way — ducted return-to-supply is the standard method, with freestanding placement as the simpler alternative — but the E130's size changes the logistics. At 98 lbs and 30" long, it's a two-person carry into a crawl space or attic, and it needs a correspondingly larger clear footprint for service access (filter changes, drain inspection). The E100's 64 lbs and 26" length is meaningfully easier for one strong person plus a helper to position.

Electrical is the same story on both: dedicated circuit recommended, hardwired variants (E100H, E130H) available for permanent installs, plug-in for the standard models. The E130's 8.3A draw vs the E100's 6.9A rarely changes the electrical scope — both land on a standard 15A or 20A dedicated circuit — but confirm with your installer if the panel is already crowded.

Drainage is gravity-only on both (no internal pump — see our [pump planning guide](/posts/crawl-space-dehumidifier-with-pump/) if your install location has no floor drain). Both ship with duct collars; the E100 Pro adds a damper in the box. Neither includes a pump, so if your mechanical room or crawl space can't drain downhill, budget the external condensate pump identically for either unit.

One more practical note: replacement filters differ in size (the E130 uses a larger 14" × 19" filter vs the E100's 13.5" × 11.9"), so they're not interchangeable if you ever own both. Both are washable MERV 8 — rinse, dry, reinstall, no disposable filter costs on either.

## Long-term ownership: 10-year math

Dehumidifiers live 8–15 years with decent care (BobVila's 2026 reporting puts quality units at 8–15 years; contractor experience clusters around 8–12). Over ten years:

- **Purchase + install:** E100 ~$2,015–2,300 + install; E130 ~$2,495–2,600 + install. Gap: ~$400–500, one time.
- **Energy:** ~$145/yr vs ~$164/yr DOE-rated — a ~$190 gap over ten years.
- **Maintenance:** Washable filters on both, annual drain-line check on both. No meaningful difference.
- **Warranty:** 5 years on both — double or triple the 1–2 years typical of budget brands, and a real part of what the premium buys.

Total 10-year ownership gap: roughly **$600–700** apart. That's real money, but it's small next to the cost of getting the capacity wrong — an undersized unit that runs constantly wears its compressor early, and a single mold remediation ($2,000–6,000+) dwarfs the price difference between these two machines ten times over.

## The verdict

Buy the E100 unless you have a specific reason to need the E130's capacity. The $400–500 savings buys the external condensate pump, the dedicated circuit work, or the duct modifications your install probably needs anyway. But if your square footage, your climate zone ([check the sizing tables](/posts/dehumidifier-sizing-by-climate-zone/)), or your gut says you're between sizes — take the E130 and never think about humidity again. In this category, the only real mistake is buying too small.
