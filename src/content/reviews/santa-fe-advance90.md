The Santa Fe Advance90 is the heavy lifter of the Santa Fe crawl space line: a 90-pint (AHAM) unit with the strongest airflow in its class, dual exhaust outlets for feeding two spaces, and a MERV-13 filter as standard. Spec sheets show 90 pints per day at 80°F/60% RH, coverage up to 3,000 square feet, and manufacture in the USA with a steel build and a 6-year warranty. The catch is the price tag — retailer listings indicate a range of roughly $1,800–3,200 depending on the supplier, which makes shopping carefully more important for this unit than for almost any other in the category.

## Capacity

The AHAM number is 90 pints/day at 80°F/60% RH, and spec sheets also quote roughly **71 pints/day at 70°F/60% RH** — a milder cold-weather penalty than the smaller Compact70, which is consistent with the larger, more powerful platform. At up to 3,000 sq ft of coverage, it's the biggest footprint of the three units in this review set.

But capacity is only half the story here. The Advance90 moves **309 CFM** — the strongest airflow in this roundup by a wide margin — which means it conditions large or oddly-shaped spaces far more effectively than lower-CFM units of similar pint ratings. Pint rating tells you how much water it pulls; CFM tells you how far that dry air travels. For sprawling crawl spaces, the combination of 90 pints and 309 CFM is the point. If you're unsure whether you need this much machine, our [AHAM vs saturation guide](/posts/aham-vs-saturation-pints-explained/) and the [capacity sizer](/tools/capacity-sizer/) will keep you honest.

## Who the Santa Fe Advance90 is for (and who should skip it)

Buy it if you have a large crawl space — up to 3,000 sq ft — or two adjacent spaces that need conditioning, because the dual exhaust outlets are a genuine differentiator: one unit, two discharge zones, no second purchase. Buy it if you want the strongest airflow available without stepping into commercial units, and if a MERV-13 stock filter and 6-year warranty matter to you. This is the premium whole-house-dryness answer for buyers who size conservatively and buy once.

Skip it if the price spread scares you — and it should give you pause. Retailer listings show a spread from ~$1,800 to ~$3,200 for the same unit, so the shopping process itself is part of the purchase: compare the exact SKU, check the seller, and don't assume the first listing you find is the market. Skip it too if installation will be a solo DIY job: at 80 lbs and 26"L x 14.5"W x 19.4"H, it needs a planned route into the crawl space, ideally with help. And if an Energy Star label is required for a rebate or program, look elsewhere — the efficiency is there, the label isn't.

## Installation and drainage

At 80 pounds, the Advance90 demands install planning: measure your access opening, plan the carry path, and bring a second person if the crawl space entry is awkward. Once placed, the dual exhaust outlets are the payoff — you can duct dry air into two zones, which owners report using for a crawl space plus a mechanical room or a split crawl space layout.

The pump is optional, not built in: Santa Fe sells a pump kit separately, and the unit otherwise drains by gravity. If your installation needs a pumped discharge — the discharge point is uphill or distant — budget for the kit up front; owners report the accessory exists but adds meaningful cost. Compare this honestly against the [AlorAir HDi90](/dehumidifiers/), whose pump is included in the base price: the Advance90 wins on build and airflow, but the total installed cost gap narrows once you add the pump kit.

## Noise, energy and running costs

Spec sheets show 59 dBA — the loudest of the three units here, which is the expected trade-off for 309 CFM of airflow. In a crawl space it's a non-issue for living areas; in a finished basement adjacent to living space, owners report it's noticeable when it kicks on.

Energy is the pleasant surprise. At **2.88 L/kWh**, the Advance90 exceeds Energy Star efficiency requirements — Santa Fe simply doesn't carry the label on this model. Electrical draw is 5.7 amps (640 watts), which is efficient for what it moves. Expect solid running costs: plug your rate and hours into the [operating cost calculator](/tools/operating-cost/), but this is a unit where the spec sheet efficiency is real, not marketing.

## Maintenance

Same maintenance story as the rest of the Santa Fe line, and a good one: the MERV-13 filter is a real pleated filter, owners report checking it quarterly and replacing as needed. No bucket to empty; gravity drain (or the optional pump) needs an occasional check for hose kinks and algae. The steel cabinet and US build quality show in the serviceability — owners report accessible internals and a long-lived platform, which is exactly what a 6-year warranty promises.

The Advance90 is the premium capacity-and-airflow pick: nothing else in the class combines 90 AHAM pints, 309 CFM, dual-zone exhaust, MERV-13 filtration, and Energy-Star-beating efficiency in a US-built steel box. The caveats are real — the wild ~$1,800–3,200 price spread means the shopping process is part of the product, the 80-pound install needs planning, the pump is an extra, and there's no Energy Star label despite qualifying efficiency.
