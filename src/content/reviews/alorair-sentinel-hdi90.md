The AlorAir Sentinel HDi90 is a crawl space dehumidifier built around one idea: a 90-pint (AHAM) unit with a built-in condensate pump at a price that undercuts most of its direct competitors. Spec sheets show it pulling 90 pints per day under the standard AHAM test conditions of 80°F and 60% relative humidity, covering up to 2,600 square feet, and retailing in the ~$1,032–1,147 range. It sits in the sweet spot between value brands that can't back a long warranty and premium American-made units that cost substantially more. The honest caveat — the "198 pints/day" on the box — gets its own section below, because it's the single most important thing to understand before you buy.

## Capacity

Here's the buyer education that matters. The Sentinel HDi90's headline capacity — 198 pints/day — is measured at saturation conditions: 90°F and 90% relative humidity. No crawl space should ever reach those conditions, and if yours did, you'd have bigger problems than a dehumidifier spec. The number that lets you compare it apples-to-apples against Santa Fe, Aprilaire, and other AHAM-rated units is **90 pints/day at 80°F/60% RH**.

To be clear: this isn't AlorAir doing anything uniquely deceptive. Saturation ratings are common in this category, and plenty of retailers list the 198 figure without context. But the HDi90 is honestly a 90-pint machine, and a strong one at that — 90 AHAM pints for roughly $1,000 with a pump included is genuinely good value. If you've been reading our [AHAM vs saturation pints explained](/posts/aham-vs-saturation-pints-explained/) guide, the HDi90 is the textbook example of why the distinction exists.

For sizing context: 90 AHAM pints covers crawl spaces and basements up to about 2,600 sq ft in normal damp conditions. Run the [capacity sizer](/tools/capacity-sizer/) if your space runs particularly wet — persistent standing water or water intrusion means you fix the drainage problem first, then size the dehumidifier for humidity control.

## Who the Sentinel HDi90 is for (and who should skip it)

Buy it if you want a 90-pint crawl space unit and you don't have a convenient gravity drain — the built-in pump is the headline reason to pick this model, and it sidesteps the single most common crawl space installation headache. It's also the pick if your budget tops out around $1,100 and you still want a 6-year warranty, which is longer coverage than most units in this price band offer.

Skip it if you need the finest stock filtration — the included MERV-1 filter is thin, and you'll want to budget for the optional MERV-8 upgrade if airborne dust matters to you. Skip it too if moving it into place will be a DIY job in a tight crawl space: at 70.5 lbs, it's the heaviest 90-pint-class unit commonly listed, so plan the haul accordingly.

## Installation and drainage

This is where the HDi90 earns its reputation. The built-in condensate pump means there's no drain line to route to daylight and no reliance on gravity fall — the pump moves water to a sink, sump, or exterior discharge point on its own. For crawl spaces where the unit sits low and the nearest drain is far or uphill, that alone can eliminate the most annoying part of the installation.

Physical dimensions are 23.2"L x 15.2"W x 17.7"H, so it fits standard crawl space heights, though it's bulkier than low-profile competitors. Owners report straightforward setup: place it, set the humidity target, route the pump discharge hose, done. An optional remote monitoring module is available for checking humidity and status without crawling back under the house — worth considering if your access opening is awkward.

## Noise, energy and running costs

Spec sheets show noise at under 58 dB, which puts it squarely in line with other 90-pint units — audible if you're standing next to it, effectively gone once you're up through the floor. Electrical draw is 5.56 amps at 115V with 210 CFM of airflow, which is respectable but not class-leading for a 90-pint machine.

On energy costs: AlorAir doesn't carry the Energy Star label on this model, so expect operating costs roughly in line with a typical 90-pint dehumidifier. Plug your local electricity rate into the [operating cost calculator](/tools/operating-cost/) to estimate the monthly bill — crawl space units run long hours in the first few months, then settle into maintenance cycles once the space is dried down. Auto-defrost operates down to 33.8°F, so it keeps running through cold months when many units would ice up and stall.

## Maintenance

The maintenance story is simple, with one caveat. The stock MERV-1 filter is the weak point — it's a coarse pre-filter, and owners report wanting something better, which is why AlorAir offers an optional MERV-8 cartridge. Plan to check the filter every few months and replace as needed; it's a cheap part and the unit is straightforward to service. The condensate pump should be checked annually for algae buildup or hose kinks, especially if it discharges infrequently in winter. Otherwise there's no bucket to empty and no daily attention required — that's the whole point of a pumped unit.

The HDi90 is our best-value crawl space pick: an honest 90-pint AHAM machine whose built-in pump solves the drainage problem that derails so many crawl space installs, backed by a 6-year warranty at a price other 90-pint units struggle to match. Just go in with eyes open — the 198 number on the box is a saturation rating, the stock filter is basic, and it's heavy to carry into a crawl space.
