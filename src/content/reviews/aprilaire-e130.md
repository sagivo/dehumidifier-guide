The AprilAire E130 is the biggest whole-home dehumidifier in this guide — a 130-pint-per-day AHAM unit (DOE 105 pints) that covers up to 7,200 sq ft. It's built in the USA, Energy Star Most Efficient certified, and designed to tie into a home's HVAC or hang dedicated in a basement, attic, or crawl space. This is the unit for large or very damp homes where smaller whole-house units would run constantly and still lose ground. Retailer listings price it in the ~$2,495–2,600 range — this is professional-grade territory, not a portable upgrade.

## Who the AprilAire E130 is for (and who should skip it)

This is for homeowners with large homes (4,000+ sq ft), consistently damp basements, or moisture loads that keep portable units running around the clock. If you're pairing it with a whole-home humidity control strategy or dealing with musty air that affects the entire house, the E130 is the reference point — our guide to [how to choose a whole-house dehumidifier](/posts/how-to-choose-whole-house-dehumidifier/) walks through when this tier becomes necessary.

Skip it if your home is under ~4,000 sq ft or your humidity problem is confined to one room or a small basement — it's genuine overkill there, and both the purchase price and the 8.3A draw reflect that. A mid-size unit or a quality portable will do the same job for less. Also skip it if you need something you can install solo in an afternoon: at 98 lbs, this needs planning.

## Capacity: the AHAM number vs the box number

Here's the good news for buyers comparing honestly: AprilAire leads with the real number. The E130 is rated at **130 pints/day under AHAM test conditions** (105 pints/day under the newer DOE standard), which is the largest residential capacity in this guide. There's no inflated saturation headline to decode — which is exactly the point our explainer on [AHAM vs saturation pints](/posts/aham-vs-saturation-pints-explained/) makes: honest AHAM numbers are the only way to compare units fairly. At up to 7,200 sq ft of coverage, this unit outranks everything else here by a wide margin.

## Installation and drainage

Spec sheets show a 30"L x 19.5"W x 18.75"H cabinet weighing 98 lbs — plan for that before the truck arrives. Owners report mounting options including hanging from joists or placing it in a utility space, with inlet/outlet collars for ducting. Gravity drain is the standard route, and the interactive digital display can be mounted on top of the unit or on the inlet side so you can read it in tight installations. Given the weight and typical integration with HVAC or plumbing, most buyers use a contractor — get the install quote alongside the equipment price, because it can add meaningfully to the total.

## Noise, energy and running costs

At 8.3A this is the hungriest unit in this guide, but the Energy Star Most Efficient certification means it's efficient *per pint removed* — the most efficient way to pull 130 pints a day is a big efficient unit, not two small ones. Run the numbers for your electricity rate with the [operating-cost calculator](/tools/operating-cost/) to see the monthly impact. Noise is generally a non-issue in practice: these units live in basements, crawl spaces, or utility rooms, not living areas — spec sheets list it as a mechanical-room appliance, and owners report it's quieter than expected for its size when installed properly.

## Maintenance

Maintenance is light: check the filter periodically and keep the drain line clear, same as any whole-home unit. The 5-year warranty is the longest coverage tier among major whole-house dehumidifiers — AprilAire backs this unit for five full years, which reflects both build quality and the made-in-USA support chain. The digital control board and humidity display are serviceable components; register the warranty at install time.

The AprilAire E130 is the honest heavyweight of residential dehumidification: the biggest AHAM-rated capacity here, a real efficiency certification, a five-year warranty, and no marketing games with the numbers. It's expensive, heavy, and more than most homes need — but if your home is large and damp enough to need it, there's nothing else in this guide that does what it does.
